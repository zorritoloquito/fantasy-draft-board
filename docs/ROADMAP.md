# Roadmap

Feature work, in priority order. Bugs live in `KNOWN-ISSUES.md`.

The 2026 draft went well — best value-per-dollar in the league (see
`DEBRIEF-2026.md`). Everything here comes from what was missing while it ran.

---

## 1. Positional scarcity and tier breaks — *highest value*

**The gap:** the board shows a per-player `PS` ("% of positional value still
below this player, undrafted") and a static `TIER`, but there is no view that
answers the question that actually drives aggression in an auction:

> How many players are left in this tier at this position, and is this the last
> one before the drop?

During the draft this had to be computed ad hoc, in chat, by querying
`live.json`. It should be on the board.

**What to build:**

- A standing per-position panel: players left, value left, % of positional value
  already gone, and the best remaining player — the table that was being
  generated by hand all draft.
- **Tier-break flagging.** Mark a player as `LAST IN TIER 2` or `TIER 2→3 CLIFF`
  when he is the final one at his position in that tier. This is the single most
  requested item: knowing a player is the last of his tier changes the correct
  bid far more than his sheet value does.
- Show the size of the drop — the value gap between him and the next tier's best
  — so "last in tier" comes with a magnitude, not just a label.

The data is already in `players.json` (`tier`, `pos`, `value`, `vor`); this is a
presentation problem, not a modeling one.

## 2. Stars-and-scrubs mode

**The gap:** the sheet encodes one strategy — flat value — and the board inherits
it. It executed well, but it structurally cannot recommend paying up for elite
players, so the entire top tier was ceded. The final roster is six RBs and five
WRs of near-identical mid-tier value, with no player above a $26 sheet value.

That was a real choice, made by the tool rather than by the drafter.

**What to build:** a strategy toggle that changes what "target price" means.

- **Value mode** (current): `target = sheet value × inflation`. Never overpay.
- **Stars-and-scrubs**: pre-allocate the budget — e.g. $140 across 2–3 elite
  targets, $60 across the remaining 12 slots — and compute target prices *within
  each bucket*. A tier-1 RB's target becomes a function of the star budget, not
  of league-wide inflation, so the board can say "go to $70" without that looking
  like an error.
- **Hybrid**: nominate a small set of must-have players who get star pricing,
  everyone else gets value pricing.

Worth showing the implied consequence live: "spending $70 here leaves $9.3/slot
for 14 spots."

## 3. Formalize the live advisor

**The gap:** the most valuable part of the draft was not the board — it was
asking questions in chat and getting answers grounded in the actual live state
("who overspent", "what's scarce", "what should I do with $58 and 6 slots").
That wasn't a designed feature; it worked because `live.json` happened to be
readable and the strategy was already understood from the sheet.

**What to build:** make it deliberate.

- A single `state` command that dumps everything an agent needs in one call:
  rosters, budgets, scarcity by position, tier breaks, inflation, recent picks.
- Save the sheet's *strategy* alongside its numbers, so an agent picking this up
  cold knows the thesis (let the room overpay early, buy the middle once
  inflation drops) rather than inferring it.
- Consider a "what should I do right now" summary rendered on the board itself
  for moments when there's no time to type.

## 4. Board reliability affordances

Small, cheap, and would have prevented most of the draft-day chaos:

- Staleness banner when `live.json` is older than ~10s (see P0-2).
- Show `soldIds` vs `filled` somewhere visible — the mismatch was the tell for
  the corruption, and it was only ever visible via the CLI.
- A "reseed from Yahoo results" button, replacing the manual scrape-and-restart
  that had to be improvised mid-draft.

## 5. Draft replay / test harness

Record every tick to disk (see P1-3), then replay a recorded draft against the
watcher. Every P0 in `KNOWN-ISSUES.md` would have been caught by replaying a
draft where someone clicks a position filter.

## 6. Smaller items

- Bye-week clustering warning. The final roster stacked four starters on bye 10
  and three on 13; nothing surfaced that until it was pointed out in chat.
- Roster-need awareness — the board never knew which lineup slots were unfilled.
- K/DEF rows in the sheet so post-draft analysis is complete.
